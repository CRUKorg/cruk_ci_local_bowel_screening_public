Methodology

This tool evaluates the impact of various socio-demographic and practice-related factors on screening coverage across England. It estimates expected screening coverage for GP practice populations, accounting for socio-demographic factors such as deprivations, age, gender, ethnicity, rurality, and practice size.

A multivariate Poisson mixed effect regression model is used to identify how these socio-demographic factors influence screening coverage. Additionally, the tool computes a Standardised Screening Ratio (SSR), which compares the observed number of screened individuals to the expected adjusted for these confounded factors. This allows for the identifying of areas (practices) with lower-than-expected uptake, even after accounting for demographic differences.

Data used

Data used in the statistical analysis were extracted for the financial year 2022/23 in England (or the latest available time period available for each dataset). GP practices for which any of the data below is not available will not be included in the primary analysis modelling.

The number of people eligible for bowel screening aged 60-74 and the number of those who took part in bowel screening in the last 30 months will be extracted for each GP practice. As FIT was phased into England’s Bowel Cancer Screening programme in June 2019​, it is it should make up the majority of this dataset, with gFOBT predominantly phased out. Deprivation scores from the Indices of Multiple Deprivation 2022 for GP practices will be extracted from the Office of Health Improvement & Disparities Public Health Data Fingertips System. Ethnic groups and MSOA codes are extracted from the Ethnicity by Middle Super Output Layer (MSOA) dataset and associated with each GP practice via its postcode, using postcode-MSOA mapping​​. Rurality group of the MSOA the GP Practice postcode is situated in will be extracted from the Rural Classification definitions available from the Department of Environment, Food & Rural Affairs​. Eight categories make up the classification system, ranging from Major Urban Conurbation (A1) to Rural Village and Dispersed in a Sparse Setting (E2).

Number of patients registered at a GP practice by age group were extracted from the NHS Digital dataset of Patients Registered at a GP Practice, May 2024​​. Number of people aged 60-74 provided information about age distribution in each practice, while proportion of males aged 60-74 served as an indicator of sex.

Analysis

A multivariate Poisson mixed effect regression model was performed with the number of persons screened as an outcome. The fixed effect predictors were deprivation score, proportion of males aged 60-74, proportion of practice population aged 60-74, practice population list size, rural/urban classification, and proportion of people who were of White British ethnicity. ICB groups were used as a random effect to account for the potential correlation among observations within the same ICB, potentially due to similar operational and healthcare delivery environments. The number of eligible people (number invited) was used as an offset. 

A Standardized Screening Ratio (SSR) was calculated as the ratio of the observed number (O) of individuals screened to the expected number (E) of individuals screened. The expected number (E) is estimated using a multivariate Poisson mixed-effects regression model, which incorporates adjustments for available confounding factors. This approach ensures that the SSR is standardized, taking into account the influence of the confounding factors within the context of the mixed-effects model. A 95% confidence interval (95% CI) of the SSR was computed using the method suggested by Vandenbroucke, 1982.
